Is debt the right choice for us, or should we raise equity?
Debt makes sense when the money has a clear, near-term return and the business can service repayments from cash flows or a planned raise: extending runway to a priced round, funding receivables, or buying assets with a multi-year payback. Equity makes sense when you are funding uncertainty. Many growth-stage companies do both, and the discovery call is where we work out the right mix for you.
Will any of these instruments dilute our equity?
Term loans, working capital lines, invoice discounting, supply chain finance, NCDs and revenue-based financing are non-dilutive. Venture debt is also non-dilutive in the ordinary sense, though lenders typically ask for a small warrant component alongside the loan. We make that trade-off explicit before you sign anything.
Which lenders are in the Orilo network?
India’s leading banks, NBFCs, fintech lenders, AIFs running venture-debt and private-credit strategies, family offices and private capital partners. Different lenders underwrite differently, so the value is less in the size of the list and more in routing your mandate to the ones built for your profile.
How does the process compress timelines?
Timelines depend on the instrument and the lender: receivable-backed lines move faster than secured term loans. What Orilo changes is sequencing. You prepare one set of numbers, lenders are approached in parallel rather than one after another, and diligence questions are handled centrally so the process does not stall on your side.
Do we need to be profitable to qualify?
Not for every instrument. Banks tend to look for a track record and security. Venture-debt funds look at the strength of your equity backers and runway. Invoice and supply-chain financiers look at the quality of your receivables and counterparties. Part of structuring is finding the lenders whose criteria you already meet.
What kind of companies does Orilo work with?
Growth-stage companies, led by founders and CFOs who want to fund growth without giving up more equity than they have to. The companies we have helped fund span logistics, HR and workforce services, SaaS, digital commerce and more.
What happens on the first call?
Fifteen minutes with Shrimay. You describe the business, the capital need and the timeline; he tells you honestly whether debt fits, which instruments are realistic, and what lenders will want to see. There is no fee for the call and no obligation afterwards.
